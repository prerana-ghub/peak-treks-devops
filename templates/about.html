What we do

Six trails, run properly, instead of sixty on a list

Every trek on this site is one we operate ourselves. We are not a marketplace reselling somebody else's batch, so the guide who meets you at the base is on our team, the permit in your name was applied for by us, and the person who answers the phone on trek morning can actually tell you where your group is.

That is also why the list is short. Each trail gets re-walked at the start of a season and the trek page gets rewritten from that walk, which is slow work. Adding a seventh trail means doing all of it again properly, so we only add one when we can.

We operate out of Indiranagar, Bengaluru, and almost everything we run is a weekend departure you can do without taking leave: a Saturday or Sunday start, back in the city the same night or by the next morning.


How we work

Things we will not do

  • Take more than fifteen trekkers in a batch, whatever the demand for a date
  • Send a batch out with one guide because the second one called in sick
  • Soften a difficulty grade to make a trail sell better
  • Collect anything extra at the trailhead or at a forest checkpost
  • Run a high trail in peak monsoon because a group has already paid
  • Light cooking fires, or carry single-use plastic up any route

A note from the founder

PEAK started the way most good weekend plans do: someone suggested a trek, a few people said yes, and suddenly we were figuring out who had a car. I liked the trekking part. I did not particularly enjoy keeping track of ten people, three different pickup points and everyone's idea of what "early morning" meant.

Somewhere along the way, those little weekend trips turned into PEAK. The idea is still pretty simple — find good trails, put together good groups, and make the whole thing easier for people who just want to go trekking without spending their entire week planning it.

I still go out on the trails whenever I can. Partly because I love it, and partly because you learn things on a trail that you won't find on a map. A route can look easy on paper and have one ridiculous climb halfway through. A "small" stream can become very much not small after rain. And sometimes the best part of a trek is the chai you find afterwards.

We're a small team, and that's intentional. We know the trails, we know the people coming on them, and we're around when things don't go exactly according to plan. Which, on a trek, they occasionally don't. That's part of the fun too.

— Prerana S, Founder

How we run a departure

The same six rules, every trek

Two guides, always

A lead guide at the front and a sweep guide at the back on every batch, regardless of size. Both carry a first-aid kit and a whistle, and a route plan is left with the office before you set off.

Headcounts at every junction

Not only at the start and the finish. On trails with side paths that is the difference between noticing someone is missing in two minutes and noticing at the summit.

Grades from a real walk

Re-walked each season. Savandurga is marked difficult because the granite is loose and exposed in three places, and if monsoon damage changes a route the grade changes before the next batch, not after.

Permits arranged in advance

Kudremukh and Kumara Parvatha sit inside reserve forest with a capped daily quota. We apply using the ID details from your booking, and the cost is already in the price you saw.

Weather calls the evening before

Everyone in the batch is told at the same time. A cancelled departure is a full refund or a free date change, your choice. A guide can also stop a group mid-trail, and that call is final.

Everything comes back down

A waste bag at the base, checked at the end rather than just handed out. No cooking fires, no single-use plastic, and rest stops kept away from water sources.

How we got here

Five years, six trails

2021

A weekend group becomes a business

PEAK registers in Bengaluru and runs its first paid departures on Skandagiri and Nandi Hills, with two guides and a borrowed van.

2022

Into the Western Ghats

Kudremukh and Kodachadri are added after a full season of route walks, along with the first forest department permit arrangements for reserve-forest trails.

2023

Granite and the hard grades

Savandurga and Kumara Parvatha go on the calendar, and the difficulty grading is rewritten so that exposure counts as much as distance.

2024

Batches capped for good

Group size is capped at fifteen across every trail and the two-guide rule becomes absolute, even when it means turning down bookings on a popular weekend.

2025

Booking moves onto this site

Dates, quotas, payment and receipts all move online, so a booking is confirmed against a real permit quota rather than a spreadsheet someone updates on Monday.
